# Self Review Cheat Sheet – R-H-G Framework
_Optimized for local LLMs (Qwen / LLaMA)_

This document defines how to write a high-quality self-review using the
**Results – How – Growth (R-H-G)** framework.

Use this as **authoritative guidance** when generating self-reviews.

---

## OVERALL GOAL

Produce a self-review that is:
- Outcome-focused (not task-focused)
- Evidence-based
- Concise and readable
- Understandable to someone outside the immediate team
- Honest and forward-looking

Avoid hype, buzzwords, or vague claims.

---

## BEFORE YOU START

Before generating the review:
- Review the role’s career framework expectations
- Identify **2–4 meaningful contributions** (not a long list)
- Prefer work with:
  - Clear impact
  - Cross-team usage
  - Production relevance
- Use evidence such as:
  - PR summaries
  - Jira context
  - Metrics (if available)

---

## RESULTS / IMPACT

### PURPOSE
Explain **what changed because of your work**.

Focus on outcomes, not activities.

### ASK YOURSELF
- What results did I deliver?
- Who benefited? (team, customers, business)
- Why did this work matter?
- What problem did it solve?

### INCLUDE
- What changed as a result of your work
- Key achievements or outcomes (not task lists)
- Scope and significance of the work
- Measurable results where possible
- If applicable, how you enabled others to succeed

### AVOID
- Listing tools, technologies, or tickets without context
- Saying “worked on”, “helped with”, “was involved in”
- Overstating impact without evidence

### STRONG LANGUAGE PATTERNS
- “Delivered ___, resulting in ___”
- “Led ___, which improved ___”
- “Enabled ___, leading to ___”
- “Reduced ___ by ___”

### EXAMPLE
**Impact:**  
Delivered X feature, improving customer adoption by 20% and reducing support tickets by 15%.

---

## HOW (Team Effectiveness & Learning Mindset)

### PURPOSE
Explain **how the results were achieved**.

Show collaboration, ownership, decision-making, and adaptability.

### ASK YOURSELF
- How did I collaborate or partner with others?
- How did I manage risk or ambiguity?
- How did I adapt when priorities changed?
- How did I unblock others or improve team velocity?
- How did I live company values?

### INCLUDE
- Collaboration and cross-functional work
- Decision-making and trade-offs
- Communication of risks and constraints
- How feedback was incorporated
- How you supported or enabled others
- Examples of ownership and accountability

### AVOID
- Repeating results already mentioned above
- Generic statements like “worked well with the team”

### STRONG LANGUAGE PATTERNS
- “Partnered with ___ to ___”
- “Aligned early with ___ to avoid ___”
- “Adjusted approach when ___”
- “Communicated risks by ___”
- “Took ownership of ___”

### EXAMPLE
**How:**  
Partnered closely with Product and Support, aligned early on requirements, and communicated risks as timelines shifted to avoid rework.

---

## GROWTH & LEARNING

### PURPOSE
Reflect honestly on **what you learned and what’s next**.

This section builds credibility.

### ASK YOURSELF
- What didn’t go as planned?
- What challenges did I face?
- What did I learn from feedback or mistakes?
- What skills or capabilities do I want to build next?
- What support would help me grow?

### INCLUDE
- One or two real challenges (without defensiveness)
- Clear lessons learned
- Skills or experiences to focus on next
- How you plan to apply learnings going forward

### AVOID
- Saying “everything went well”
- Framing weaknesses as strengths
- Overly vague growth goals

### STRONG LANGUAGE PATTERNS
- “I learned that ___”
- “This highlighted the need to ___”
- “Going forward, I’m focused on ___”
- “I would benefit from ___”

### EXAMPLE
**Growth:**  
Learned the importance of earlier stakeholder alignment and am focusing on improving prioritization and executive communication next year.

---

## FINAL REVIEW CHECK (BEFORE SUBMITTING)

Confirm that:
- Impact is clear within the **first sentence**
- Content is concise and readable
- Statements are specific, not vague
- Results and behaviors are both covered
- Tone is confident but not inflated
- Review makes sense to someone outside the team

---

## IMPORTANT LLM INSTRUCTIONS

When generating a review:
- Prefer **clarity over verbosity**
- Prefer **evidence over adjectives**
- Prefer **outcomes over effort**
- Do not invent metrics
- Do not exaggerate scope
- Do not use marketing language
